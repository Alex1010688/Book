17  Useful other sources of information

Different resources answer different questions: how equipment works, what a task requires, what applies at a location, or what a technical term means. The following are useful starting points for study and day-to-day reference.

17.1 OFFICIAL DOCUMENTATION

Signal maintenance specifications (SMS)

The Signal Maintenance Specifications describe the maintenance tasks and technical requirements for signalling equipment. Use the applicable specification to establish what must be inspected, maintained or measured, together with the relevant limits and recording requirements.

Signal maintenance testing handbook (SMTH)

The Signal Maintenance Testing Handbook sets out the processes, checks and tests for signalling maintenance testing within its defined scope. It helps establish the testing required after work such as equipment replacement, together with the associated planning and records.

Network Rail standards

Network Rail standards set out requirements for engineering, maintenance, safety and the management of railway assets. Use the controlled standards system to check the applicable document, issue and compliance date.

RSSB rulebook

The RSSB Rule Book contains the operational rules, modules and handbooks relevant to railway roles. For an S&T technician, it explains responsibilities and arrangements for working on signalling equipment and communicating with those controlling train movements.

Sectional Appendix

The Sectional Appendix provides route-specific information, including track layouts, line names, mileages, permissible speeds and local instructions. It is useful for understanding the railway at a particular location and relating equipment to its operational setting.

Hazard Directory

The National Hazard Directory records known hazards and location information to support planning and working on Network Rail infrastructure. Consult the relevant entries alongside the site-specific information and arrangements for the work.

Course notes

Course notes provide explanations, diagrams and worked examples from formal training, making them useful for revision and refreshing equipment knowledge. Check any task requirements or numerical limits against the applicable controlled documentation.

Network Rail e-learning

Network Rail e-learning provides online learning modules and assessments to support training, briefings and revision. Use the modules relevant to your role to prepare for courses or revisit topics that need further study.

eB suite

The eB suite is used to store, manage and retrieve engineering documents and records, including drawings. Check the document identity, revision and status to establish that the record is appropriate for the equipment and work being considered.

17.2 ONLINE RESOURCES

railwaycodes.org

Railway Codes is a site which lists all codes used on the railway. It’s useful for looking up ELRs.

dickthesignals

dickthesignals contains a collection of signalling drawings, diagrams, manuals and technical documents that can help explain equipment and principles. Use it for background study, checking practical requirements against controlled documentation.

Glasgow S&T SharePoint

Use the Glasgow S&T SharePoint site to find local team information and shared reference material. Check the status and revision of any document before using it for a task.

17.3 PHYSICAL RESOURCES

Ellis’ engineering encyclopaedia

Ellis’ British Railway Engineering Encyclopaedia explains railway engineering terms, abbreviations, acronyms and jargon across several disciplines. It is a useful desk reference when an unfamiliar word appears in a drawing, document or conversation.
